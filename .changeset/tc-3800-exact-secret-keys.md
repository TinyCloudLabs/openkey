One secret's own key. A delegation may name `urn:tinycloud:encryption:<owner>:default/secrets/<name>` or `…:default/secrets/scoped/<scope>/<name>` for `tinycloud.encryption/decrypt` and `tinycloud.encryption/key.create`, in the signer's own account only: the owner must be the signer's canonical (EIP-55) address and every path part must match `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63}`. Another path under `default/secrets/` is refused with "permissions[i].path names an invalid secret key; it must be default/secrets/<name> or default/secrets/scoped/<scope>/<name>, each part matching [a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63}". `key.create` stays refused everywhere else. `/device` still refuses secrets.

The review names the secret: "Read the secret NAME" (from the KV path the request pairs with the key, otherwise the key's own name), and "Create a key for the new secret NAME." with "It can't read any of your secrets." under it. A request that can open every secret gets the all-secrets card: "Let {site} read and add all your secrets?", "Let {site} read all your secrets?" or "Let {site} open all your encrypted data?", with "this app" and "OpenKey can't confirm which site asked." where no site is verified. On the warning screen it replaces the whole-network card; on a link or the command line it sits above the summary.

The all-secrets card also covers `decrypt` or `key.create` on `default/secrets` itself (no trailing slash), on any prefix under `secrets/` (`secrets/scoped/`, `secrets/scoped/<scope>/`) and on an encryption path OpenKey cannot read that names `secrets` or a path under it once lower-cased and percent-decoded (`Secrets/`, `SECRETS/x`, `secrets%2F`). Each starts at one hour and lasts 7 days at most; none shows the one-secret card. The lower-casing and decoding only pick the card; they don't change the signed bytes.

A signed approval request (`/a/…`) may be lengthened past its suggested start up to the lifetime its requester signed, unless it can open all secrets or all encrypted data (that start stays the most). A request that may `decrypt` or `key.create` on a secret's own key or a prefix under `secrets/`, `decrypt` a whole network, read the secrets vault in the KV store (`get`, `list`, `metadata` or `sync` on `vault/secrets` or a path under it) or use the `secrets` service lasts 7 days at most, whatever it signed: the page offers no more and the API refuses a longer expiry. A request for a secret's own key says "This request starts at {start}, as the app asked. You can choose up to {max}." or, when it starts at its maximum, "This request lasts {max}, as the app asked. That's the most it can be."

Every other path holds a grant that may `decrypt` or `key.create` on a key or prefix under `default/secrets/`, read the secrets vault in the KV store or use the `secrets` service to 7 days too. The /delegate popup and pasted link (`/prepare`, the managed `POST /delegate`) and the frame (`/authorize-sign-preview`) bring a longer expiry, the app's or the owner's, back to 7 days from now; the popup, frame and command-line screens offer no more. Signed bytes that still ask for more are refused with 400 `secret_lifetime_exceeded` and "This request asks for secret access longer than 7 days. Secret access lasts at most 7 days, so ask again with an expiry of 7 days or less.": `/complete`, `/authorize-sign`, raw `/api/keys/:id/sign` (text or hex) and the OAuth `/api/delegate/sign` paths. A whole network's `decrypt` is held the same way outside `/a/` only while `OPENKEY_NETWORK_DECRYPT_CAP=enabled` (off by default); its refusal reads "This request asks for access to secrets or encrypted data longer than 7 days. Access to secrets or encrypted data lasts at most 7 days, so ask again with an expiry of 7 days or less." An encryption resource that cannot be parsed, or a ReCap that cannot be read, counts as reaching secrets.

The web's popup, frame and command-line screens follow the same flag through `VITE_OPENKEY_NETWORK_DECRYPT_CAP=enabled`, set together with the API's; `docker-compose.prod.yml` passes `OPENKEY_NETWORK_DECRYPT_CAP` to the API (empty unless set).
